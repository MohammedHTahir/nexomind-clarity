## Monday — Founder insight

```
I deleted the streak counter from my journaling app.

Users got calmer. Daily use went up.

Streaks gamify the wrong thing. They reward consistency, not honesty. People wrote to maintain the streak instead of because they had something to process.

Naming the loop > marking the day.
```

**Why this works:** Counterintuitive opening line. Specific result. Names
the mechanic. Closes on the principle.

```
Overthinking isn't a thinking problem.

It's a naming problem.

Here's what I mean ↓
```

**Post 2:**
```
A thought loops because the feeling underneath it hasn't been named in a single word.

You can write 3 pages about a hard conversation and still not know if you're holding hurt, or pressure, or fear of being misunderstood.

The loop runs because the emotion isn't identified.

```
Once you name the emotion, the loop has somewhere to land.

Not "felt." Not "expressed." Named. In one word.

The brain is doing accounting. Until the bill has a label, it can't be filed.

That label is the work.

## What to do after posting each one

1. **First 30 minutes after posting:** reply to anyone who replies to
   you. X's algorithm boosts posts with fast author engagement.
2. **Don't delete a post that flops.** Even bombing posts contribute to
   your account's "topic signal" — X learns what your content is about.
3. **Repost (with quote) the ones that perform** the next week with a new
   angle. This is the most underused tactic on X for low-follower
   accounts.

## Engagement homework (10 min/day)

Before posting your own content each day, find 5 accounts in this list
and leave one specific, useful reply on their most recent post:

- Therapists who post on X (search: "therapist", "anxiety", "overthinking")
- Mental-health-adjacent founders (Calm, Headspace, Reflectly, Daylio teams)
- Productivity creators who talk about journaling
- Small founder accounts (<10k) building in adjacent spaces

The goal is not promotion. The goal is being a real person in the niche.
Replies that add a specific thought get noticed. Replies that say "great
post!" do not.
