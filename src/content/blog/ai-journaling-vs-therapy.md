A question we get often: "Is AI journaling supposed to replace therapy?"

No. It's not built to. But the question reveals something interesting — most people don't actually know what each is *for*. They sound similar. They're not.

## What therapy is for

Therapy is a structured, long-term relationship with a trained clinician. Its job is to help you understand patterns across years, work through trauma, and rewire how you relate to yourself and others. A good therapist sees what you can't.

Therapy is the right tool when:
- A pattern keeps repeating across relationships, jobs, or decades
- You're processing trauma, grief, or a major life rupture
- You need a diagnosis, treatment plan, or medication
- You want a long-term ally in your mental health

## What AI journaling is for

AI journaling is a daily, in-the-moment tool. Its job is to help you process the thought you're having *right now* — the one looping at 2pm or 1am — and turn it into something you can put down.

AI journaling is the right tool when:
- You're stuck in a thought loop and need to break it
- You want to make sense of a feeling before talking to anyone about it
- It's between sessions and something is brewing
- You need a private space to think out loud, without judgment

## The clearest analogy

Therapy is the cardiologist. AI journaling is the daily walk. You don't visit a cardiologist every time your heart rate goes up. And you don't replace the cardiologist with a walk. They cover different ground.

## Why "private" matters

Therapy is confidential because the therapist is bound by law and ethics. AI journaling has to be *technically* private — encrypted, never sold, never used to train models. If those guarantees aren't there, neither is the tool. NexoMind treats privacy as a precondition, not a feature.

Use both. They're not competing for the same job.
